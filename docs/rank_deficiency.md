# Rank-deficient designs and the downstream least-squares learner

Verification record, 2026-11-13. Script:
`tests/fixtures/cross_language/rank_deficiency_study.py`. R reference:
`tests/fixtures/cross_language/r_reference.json`, generated by running
`sharedimp-r` at commit `92f9ecb` (R 4.4.3; built-in `"linear"` learner =
`lm.fit` with aliased coefficients set to 0).

## Question

On data with exactly collinear features, ordinary least squares gave different
`shared_importance()` results depending on the scikit-learn / NumPy version,
and different from R. Is that a disagreement in UMFI-LR, or in the learner?

## Answer

**In the learner.** The residualization is identical in R and Python on every
design below: slopes, intercepts, collinearity flags and the Full/Reduced
training and assessment matrices agree to `1e-8` (in fact to about `1e-15`;
`tests/test_cross_language.py::test_representations_and_predictions_match_r`).
What differs is how a least-squares routine treats a design matrix that is
not of full column rank. It is therefore not treated as a UMFI-LR
disagreement, and the package does not alter the user's estimator.

## Where rank deficiency comes from

Rule D3 of the specification removes collinearity *with the anchor* (the
residual of such a feature is exactly zero, a zero column). Two situations
leave the Full or Reduced design rank-deficient:

1. **A zero column** (a feature flagged by D3, or a feature that is constant on
   the training rows, D2). Harmless: its coefficient is 0 under every
   convention.
2. **Two features collinear with each other but not with the anchor.** After
   residualization on a third anchor they are still collinear, up to round-off
   (smallest/largest singular value `≤ 1.2e-16`).
3. **An anchor that is constant on the training rows** (D1). The raw anchor
   stays in the Full design and is collinear with the intercept there, but
   *varies on the assessment rows*.

## What the measurements show

* **Full-rank designs are unaffected.** Every solver agrees with R to round-off.
* **A solver must recognise the rank deficiency.** If it inverts a singular
  value of order `1e-16` (`rcond=1e-20`), round-off is amplified into the
  predictions and the scores are wrong by up to many orders of magnitude.
* **Library defaults sit at the edge.** The critical singular value is at
  machine epsilon, the same order as the default cut-offs. `LinearRegression`
  got one cell wrong on the older stack and none on the newer one;
  `scipy.linalg.lstsq` with its default got one cell wrong on *both* stacks;
  `numpy.linalg.lstsq` got none wrong on either. Which routine fails where is
  not something to rely on.
* **Recognising the deficiency is not enough when the assessment rows leave
  the dependency.** For an anchor that is constant on the training rows, the
  training fit is the same for every least-squares solution, but the
  assessment rows do not satisfy "anchor = constant". R's `lm.fit` aliases the
  anchor column (coefficient 0, the anchor is ignored); a minimum-norm
  solution spreads the intercept over the constant column and the intercept
  and therefore reacts to the anchor's assessment values. Both are valid
  least-squares solutions; their predictions differ (here by `1.5e-2` in the
  score). `LinearRegression` happens to agree with R in this case because it
  centres the columns first, which turns the constant column into zeros.

## Consequences

* **For users:** nothing changes. `shared_importance()` passes the
  representations to the estimator it was given and never substitutes another
  solver. A learner that is unstable on collinear inputs is unstable with or
  without sharedimp; regularised or tree-based learners are not affected in
  this way.
* **For cross-language parity tests:** rank-deficient designs are fitted with
  `AliasingOLS` (defined in `tests/test_cross_language.py`), a small
  deterministic test-only learner that reproduces R's convention: columns of
  `[1, X]` are taken in order, a column whose component orthogonal to the
  accepted columns is at most `1e-7` of its norm is aliased (coefficient 0),
  the rest is fitted by least squares. With it, all four designs agree with R
  to `1e-8` on both stacks. Full-rank designs keep `LinearRegression`.
* **For the common specification:** these findings are incorporated in spec
  §8.1 and §15.4 since `sharedimp-r` commit `cf2e1cc`: predictions on
  assessment rows that leave the training dependency depend on the solver's
  convention, recognising the rank deficiency is necessary but not sufficient
  to reproduce `lm.fit`, and parity tests on such designs must reproduce R's
  aliasing convention.

## Not examined

Other rank-deficient learners (unpenalized logistic regression on collinear
designs), and platforms other than Linux x86-64 with the BLAS/LAPACK builds
shipped in the NumPy/SciPy wheels of the two stacks.
